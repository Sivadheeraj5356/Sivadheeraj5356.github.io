NOTES ON HUMAN-AI INTERACTION · 10 / 10

Confidence is a visual problem

Chat renders a guess and a certainty in the same font. That is a design failure, not a model failure.

Human speech carries calibration for free: "I think", "obviously", "no idea, but". A language model produces the same fluent declarative sentence at 99 percent confidence and at 51. The transcript launders uncertainty into authority, and the interface does nothing to repair it. So users learn calibration the hard way: by being burned, then by trusting nothing, which is its own failure.

Confidence belongs in the rendering

Not in the prompt, in the pixels. Lower-confidence claims in lighter ink. Claims linked to their sources when sources exist, and visibly sourceless when none do. The reader should be able to feel the epistemics of a paragraph before evaluating a single word of it.

Progressive disclosure of doubt

Answer first, caveats one tap away: "what I might be missing." The user who wants the headline gets the headline. The user about to bet money gets the fold. And sometimes the right rendering is the honest ellipsis: "I do not know, and here is what would settle it."

An AI that can say that legibly will be trusted with more, not less. The interface's job is not to make the model look certain. It is to make certainty and doubt readable at a glance, so that trust lands exactly where it was earned.
