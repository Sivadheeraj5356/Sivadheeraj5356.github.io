NOTES ON HUMAN-AI INTERACTION · 02 / 10

Software that knocks

Proactivity is a privilege agents hold at the user's pleasure, and the penalty for bad manners is being muted.

Every AI product roadmap now includes the word proactive. Almost none of them have earned it. Proactivity is a privilege an agent holds at the user's pleasure, and the penalty for abusing it is not a wrong answer. It is being muted, and a muted agent is a dead one.

Good colleagues already follow rules we never wrote down. Interrupt rarely. Match the interruption to the stakes. Always carry your reason with you. Software that wants to work alongside us has to learn the same manners, and manners are an interface problem, not a model problem.

Three tiers of interruption

The log: silent, reviewable later, where most agent output belongs. The badge: visible but undemanding, for things worth knowing today. The knock: rare, reserved for the few moments where waiting has a real cost. The whole art is in the routing, and the routing should be teachable: one tap on "never for this reason" sets a boundary without a settings page.

Every proactive message should carry its own receipt: why now, what changed, what happens if you ignore it. "Why are you telling me this?" is a question the interface should answer before you think to ask it.

Budgets, not toggles

Attention is the resource being spent, so treat it like money. Quiet hours should be inferred from behavior, not configured with checkboxes. If I never open your 7 AM summary, the summary should start arriving at 9 without me filing a ticket about it.

The agents that win the next few years will not be the smartest ones. They will be the ones you would leave a key with.
