NOTES ON HUMAN-AI INTERACTION · 06 / 10

Steering wheels, not prompts

We gave agents engines and left them a destination field with no wheel, no mirrors and no brakes.

Prompting is a one-shot declaration of intent. Steering is continuous control. The gap between them is where most agent products fail: the user watches a ten-minute run go wrong in minute two, and the only corrective available is to stop everything and rephrase the original wish.

The intervention ladder

Corrections exist at four rungs: the goal, the plan, the step, the tool call. Most failures get corrected at the wrong rung. Users rewrite the entire prompt when they wanted to veto one step, because the product gave them no place to veto a step. Each rung needs its own handle.

Legibility before control

You cannot steer what you cannot see. A live plan view - here is what I am doing, here is what comes next - turns the user from passenger into driver. Interrupting should be cheaper than redoing: every second of agent work raises the cost of the correction it has not yet received. The brake pedal belongs within one tap, always, and "redirect" should fork the plan from the current point rather than restart it from scratch.

Corrections should compound

A correction made twice is a preference. "Never email anyone before 9 AM" said once is a patch; the interface should offer to make it a standing rule. Agents that treat steering as noise to route around will keep crashing in the same places. Agents that treat it as curriculum get easier to drive every week.
