NOTES ON HUMAN-AI INTERACTION · 04 / 10

Voice is not a transcript

Speech is the richest input we have, and the standard pipeline strips it for parts.

The standard voice pipeline is speech to text, text to chatbot, chatbot to text, text to speech. At every arrow, something is thrown away. The hesitation before a yes. The emphasis that flips a meaning. The mid-sentence language switch that tells you where the emotion lives.

A hesitant "haan" and a firm "haan" transcribe to the same word. They should not get the same response. The first deserves "want me to walk through the options?", the second deserves action. An interface that cannot hear the difference understood the words and missed the message.

Turn-taking is the whole game

Humans interrupt each other constantly and gracefully. Barge-in is not rudeness; it is how a conversation breathes. An agent that cannot be interrupted mid-sentence is not a conversationalist, it is a lecture with a microphone. The cost of interrupting should be modeled and made visible: cheap while the agent is listing options, expensive mid-commitment.

India is the stress test

Hundreds of millions of people speak better than they type, and they speak in two languages at once. Code-mixed Hinglish is not an edge case to tolerate; it is the default to design for. Voice is the only mainstream interface that does not demand the user first become literate in the machine's medium. Whoever gets prosody, interruption and code-switching right does not just win a feature. They unlock the next half-billion users.

The microphone hears more than words. Interfaces that listen only to the transcript are deaf in one ear.
